## Executive Diagnosis

Transparent eCom has significant activity and automation, but the main constraint is not lack of channels. It is conversion clarity and operating discipline between attention, qualification, booking, attendance, sales progression, and revenue.

The current system should be improved in this order:

1. Make the offer, audience, proof, and compliance claims unmistakably credible.
2. Make one booking path the canonical conversion path.
3. Make every lead, meeting, owner, outcome, and revenue event measurable.
4. Improve speed-to-lead, attendance, qualification, and follow-up execution.
5. Scale only the channels and segments that produce qualified pipeline, not activity alone.

Do not solve the problem by immediately sending more email, increasing Vapi volume, or adding more automations. Those actions can increase cost, complaints, operational load, and attribution noise before the funnel is understood.

The core offer should be positioned as **compliance infrastructure plus regulated-growth execution**, not generic digital marketing and not a promise that ads will never be rejected. The distinctive capability is the combination of:

- Access to specialized compliance ad-account infrastructure for regulated categories.
- Practical knowledge of platform rules, regulated-product language, creative, landing pages, and account operations.
- Vetting and operating discipline that reduce avoidable policy and account-risk mistakes.
- Optional full-service execution, rather than forcing every prospect into a self-service tool.
- Measurement from approved ads through qualified demand, booked meetings, pipeline, and revenue.

## Customer FAQ and Objection Library

This FAQ should be published as an approved source for the website, chatbot, email, ads, SDR calls, and meeting confirmations. Answers should be adapted to the prospect's segment, but the underlying claims and boundaries must remain consistent.

### Why Transparent eCom?

**Why do I need Transparent eCom if I can run ads myself?**

You may be able to run ordinary campaigns yourself. The reason to involve Transparent eCom is that regulated businesses often need more than campaign setup: they need the right account infrastructure, policy-aware creative and destinations, a review process, and measurement that connects advertising to qualified demand. We help identify which parts you can own internally and where specialized support may reduce avoidable risk or wasted effort.

**What can Transparent eCom do that I cannot do myself?**

We bring specialized experience with regulated categories, compliance-oriented ad-account infrastructure, policy-aware campaign operations, and the related landing-page, CRM, and follow-up systems. You can choose self-service, hybrid, or full-service support. The value is not that the work is impossible to do internally; it is that Transparent eCom has a focused operating system for a difficult category and can help you avoid learning every lesson through rejected campaigns or disrupted acquisition.

**Are you an ad agency, a technology provider, or a compliance consultant?**

We combine those functions around regulated customer acquisition. Depending on fit, we can provide specialized account access, strategy, creative and campaign support, measurement, CRM and follow-up coordination, or full-service execution. The assessment determines which layer is actually needed.

**What makes a compliance ad account different from a normal ad account?**

A specialized account is operated with additional category knowledge, policy controls, review discipline, and platform-specific processes suited to regulated advertising. It is not a waiver of platform rules and it does not make prohibited claims or products automatically acceptable. The account is one part of a broader compliance-and-growth process.

**Can I use real cannabis, CBD, mushroom, or other regulated-product language and imagery?**

Possibly, depending on the platform, jurisdiction, product, claim, creative, destination, and account setup. We assess the specific use case rather than promise blanket approval. The prospect remains responsible for truthful claims, lawful products, required disclosures, and compliance with applicable rules.

**Can you guarantee that my ads will be approved or that my account will not be banned?**

No. Platform decisions are outside our control. We can help reduce avoidable risk through appropriate infrastructure, policy-aware preparation, testing, monitoring, and response procedures, but we do not guarantee approval, account continuity, performance, revenue, or timing.

### What happens next?

**What can I expect from my meeting with Cameron?**

Expect a practical assessment, not a generic sales presentation. Cameron will review your business category, products and claims, current acquisition problem, account and campaign history, landing pages or destinations, tracking, and growth constraints. He will explain which capabilities may apply, show relevant past work where approved, identify limitations, and recommend a next step only if there is a credible fit.

**What should I bring to the meeting?**

Bring your primary products or offers, target markets, current advertising channels, recent approval or rejection examples if available, landing-page URLs, approximate spend and goals, and the person responsible for marketing or acquisition. Do not send passwords or sensitive account credentials before they are requested through an approved process.

**Will you audit my ad account during the first meeting?**

The first meeting is a scoped assessment. We can review the information and examples you provide, but a complete account audit may require a separate engagement, access process, or technical review. We should never imply that an account was audited when it was not.

**What will I receive after the meeting?**

If there is a fit, you should receive a concise summary of the observed constraint, recommended workstreams, prerequisites, available service model, measurement plan, and next decision. If there is not a fit, we should say so clearly and avoid forcing a proposal.

**How long does it take to see results?**

There is no responsible universal timeline. Timing depends on account status, product and claim review, creative and destination readiness, audience, budget, platform decisions, tracking, and fulfillment capacity. We can define milestones after the assessment, but should not promise a launch date or revenue outcome in advance.

### Fit, service models, and commercial questions

**Do I need full-service management?**

No. Self-service may be appropriate when your team can manage creative, campaigns, landing pages, compliance review, and measurement. Hybrid support can fill specific gaps. Full-service is appropriate when you want Transparent eCom to coordinate more of the strategy and execution. The right model depends on capability, urgency, risk, and internal ownership.

**Who is a good fit for Transparent eCom?**

A good fit is a regulated business with a real acquisition problem, a lawful and clearly defined offer, a decision-maker or appropriate marketing owner, realistic expectations, and the ability to support the recommended work. We prioritize fit and delivery capacity over adding every possible regulated business.

**Who is not a good fit?**

We are not a fit for businesses seeking guaranteed approval, guaranteed revenue, a way around platform rules, promotion of prohibited products or claims, or a replacement for legal or regulatory advice. We should also decline work when the offer, destination, data, budget, or fulfillment capacity is not ready.

**How much does it cost?**

Pricing depends on the service model, scope, account and campaign complexity, creative and landing-page needs, media budget, and measurement requirements. The assessment should clarify the recommended scope and prerequisites before a price is presented. Do not use a low entry price to hide an undefined delivery burden.

**Do you manage the ad spend?**

Service fees and media spend should be explained separately. The proposal should state who funds the media, who owns the account and data, what access is required, what is included in management, and what happens if a platform pauses or rejects activity.

**Can you work with my existing agency or marketing team?**

Yes, when responsibilities and access are clear. We can provide specialized account support, a review layer, strategy, or a defined workstream while your team retains other responsibilities. The operating plan must identify one owner for each deliverable and prevent conflicting changes.

### Trust, proof, and risk

**Can you show me proof that this works?**

We can show approved, relevant examples with the starting problem, work performed, time period, denominator, result, and limitations. Proof should be matched to your segment and service model. A logo or anecdote alone is not sufficient evidence of a likely outcome.

**What happens if an ad is rejected?**

We review the rejection context, creative, copy, destination, account signals, and available platform process; then we recommend a measured response. A rejection is not automatically proof that the platform is wrong or that a workaround is appropriate. We document the decision and avoid repeated uncontrolled submissions.

**What information do you need from me?**

We need accurate information about the business, products, claims, target markets, destinations, advertising history, account ownership, goals, and internal responsibilities. We do not need passwords in an introductory conversation. Misleading or incomplete information can invalidate the assessment.

**How do you handle my customer and advertising data?**

The proposal should define access, ownership, permitted uses, retention, security responsibilities, reporting, and offboarding. We should request only the access necessary for the agreed work and document any third-party platform limitations.

### Booking and follow-up questions

**Why is the first step a meeting instead of a proposal?**

The right recommendation depends on the category, offer, account history, destination, internal capability, and commercial fit. A short assessment prevents us from selling an unsuitable service or making a claim without enough context.

**What happens if I am not ready to buy?**

The meeting can still clarify the main constraint, prerequisites, and the next useful step. We should provide an honest answer, record the appropriate follow-up date, and respect a request not to be contacted.

**Can I reschedule or cancel?**

Yes. Confirmation and reminder messages should provide a clear reschedule and cancellation path. The CRM should record the appointment outcome accurately as showed, no-show, canceled, or rescheduled.

### How to use the FAQ in messaging

- Website: use the first six questions near the video and booking CTA; use the service-model and fit questions below the offer comparison.
- Email: turn one question into the subject or opening, answer it in three to five sentences, and offer the checklist or assessment as the single next step.
- Ads: use the question as the hook, make one bounded claim about the mechanism, and link to the capability brief rather than promising approval or performance.
- SDR calls: use the prospect's stated concern to select one answer; do not recite the entire FAQ or diagnose an unverified failure.
- Booking confirmation: answer what will happen in the meeting, what to prepare, and what will not happen, including no guarantee of approval or revenue.
- Chatbot: answer only from this approved library, ask one clarifying question at a time, and route complex account or legal questions to the assessment rather than improvising.
- Sales meeting: use the FAQ as a checklist for objections, then update the proof, claim, and disposition records when a repeated question reveals a messaging gap.

**FAQ acceptance gate:** every answer must have an approved owner, source or proof reference where applicable, review date, and explicit boundary for what Transparent eCom can and cannot control.

## Department Recommendations

This plan is organized by role. It does not assign named individuals, authorize a pilot, or authorize production changes. Each role should complete the planning and review work below before requesting implementation.

### Website and Landing-Page Administration

**Purpose:** make the public conversion path clear, credible, measurable, and maintainable.

**Recommendations:**

- Inventory every live page, landing page, form, calendar, CTA, tracking parameter, thank-you page, legal link, and embedded widget.
- Designate one canonical Regulated Ads booking path and state the timezone as Pacific Time wherever the prospect sees scheduling information.
- Remove or clearly secondary-position competing calendars, legacy forms, Calendly routes, placeholder links, and unrelated primary CTAs.
- Maintain a page-by-page content matrix covering audience, problem, offer, proof, CTA, owner, last review date, and deployment status.
- Keep claims tied to an approved claim-and-proof matrix. Replace unsupported guarantees with diagnostic, process, or risk-reduction language.
- Add a release checklist covering mobile layout, form submission, booking creation, source retention, consent, privacy, confirmation, reschedule, no-show recovery, and accessibility.
- Use versioned backups and a documented rollback path for every website or landing-page deployment.
- Review conversion-path health weekly using CTA clicks, booking starts, completed bookings, booking-to-contact matches, and unattributed traffic.

**Required outputs:** website inventory, canonical journey map, page content matrix, claim/proof matrix, QA checklist, analytics specification, and rollback procedure.

**Do not proceed when:** any primary CTA has a blank destination, the booking calendar is ambiguous, required legal or consent content is missing, or a page depends on placeholder configuration.

### Automation Operations

**Purpose:** keep CRM, n8n, messaging, reporting, and data flows reliable, auditable, and safe to change.

**Recommendations:**

- Maintain a live workflow registry containing workflow ID, purpose, trigger, schedule, owner role, dependencies, credentials, side effects, pause state, and rollback reference.
- Treat live n8n state as authoritative. Fetch the current workflow before every change, patch the smallest safe surface, then verify the published version and active version match.
- Separate read-only diagnostics, dry-run tests, controlled tests, and production executions. Never use a live sender as a test harness.
- Standardize webhook authentication, credential ownership, Config-node use where required, retry behavior, timeouts, rate-limit handling, idempotency, and fail-closed behavior.
- Define data contracts for contacts, opportunities, appointments, campaign events, source attribution, owner attribution, and suppression states.
- Monitor scheduled executions, failed executions, stale source health, queue growth, duplicate sends, missing release-log rows, and attribution coverage.
- Use shared incident alerts with actionable failure context, but keep automatic remediation limited to approved, reversible infrastructure fixes.
- Maintain a dependency map so a website, CRM, calendar, sender, or reporting change can be impact-checked before deployment.
- Document every production mutation with the reason, pre-change state, changed fields, verification result, and recovery path.

**Required outputs:** workflow registry, data-contract register, monitoring checklist, incident runbook, credential inventory, change template, and rollback log.

**Do not proceed when:** the live graph has not been fetched, credentials are unclear, a mutation cannot be verified, or a proposed test would send, tag, enroll, call, or otherwise alter live records.

### Executive Report and Measurement

**Purpose:** give leadership a small set of trustworthy numbers that lead to a decision. The report should not be a warehouse dump or a collection of metrics that cannot be explained, trusted, or acted upon.

**Primary dashboard: keep only decision-grade metrics**

- Qualified meetings booked with Cameron, measured from appointments by `start_at`.
- Showed, no-show, canceled, and rescheduled meetings, with outcome-completion rate.
- SQLs created, MQL-to-SQL conversion, open qualified pipeline, won revenue, and lost volume.
- SDR attribution for booked meetings and SQLs, with an explicit `Unassigned` bucket.
- Lead source attribution for MQLs, SQLs, and qualified meetings, with attributed/total coverage.
- Speed-to-lead, follow-up completion, and next-action overdue counts.
- Source and workflow health: freshness, last successful run, row counts, and failed-run status.
- A short action queue: meetings missing outcomes, bookings missing SDR attribution, opportunities missing owners or next steps, stale sources, and suppressed/duplicate records requiring repair.

**Move out of the primary dashboard:**

- Raw contact counts without qualification or identity coverage.
- Email opens, clicks, impressions, social engagement, and page sessions unless tied to a defined decision or funnel step.
- Large campaign, UTM, stage, call, and social breakdowns that do not change an assigned action.
- Metrics sourced from incomplete snapshots without a visible coverage warning.
- Any metric whose definition, date window, owner, or source table cannot be stated in one sentence.

**Data-quality rules:**

- Every metric must display its definition, date window, source, last refresh, and coverage percentage.
- If coverage is incomplete, show `Partial` or `Unavailable` rather than a precise-looking number.
- Do not combine appointment-based bookings with opportunity-stage bookings in one KPI.
- Do not infer showed/no-show from assumptions; require an explicit GHL appointment outcome.
- Do not rank SDRs or channels when attribution coverage is too low to support a fair comparison.
- Keep detailed raw breakdowns available in a separate diagnostic view or export, not in the leadership summary.

**Required report layout:**

1. **Decisions:** qualified bookings, shows, SQLs, pipeline, revenue, and period-over-period movement.
2. **Ownership and source:** SDR attribution, lead source, and coverage warnings.
3. **Action queue:** the records or system conditions that need work this week.
4. **Health:** freshness, failed sources, incomplete data, and known limitations.

**Report acceptance gate:** a metric remains on the primary dashboard only if leadership can answer what changed, why it changed, who acts next, and whether the underlying data is sufficiently complete. Otherwise it belongs in diagnostics or should be removed.

### Ownership Guidance

**Purpose:** create one accountable sales operating model from lead qualification through fulfillment.

**Recommendations:**

- Define the approved ICP, segment priority, disqualifiers, commercial-fit thresholds, offer scope, pricing logic, prerequisites, and minimum margin.
- Define the owner authority chain for contacts, opportunities, appointments, and follow-up. Conflicts must be visible and routed for review, not silently overwritten.
- Use one qualification rubric across forms, SDRs, Vapi, booking workflows, and the sales assessment.
- Define booked, showed, no-show, MQL, SQL, opportunity, won, lost, and revenue terms before using them in scorecards.
- Require every qualified meeting to have a source, campaign, owner, reason for contact, qualification summary, meeting outcome, and next action.
- Set Pacific Time response, reminder, reschedule, no-show recovery, and post-meeting SLAs that the team can actually staff.
- Maintain a proof library with baseline, date range, denominator, result, client permission, limitations, and review date.
- Protect fulfillment capacity. Acquisition volume must stop or narrow when onboarding, delivery quality, gross margin, or customer-success capacity is at risk.
- Run a weekly pipeline review focused on qualified outcomes, stage movement, next steps, risk, and revenue rather than raw activity.

**Required outputs:** ICP and disqualifier brief, offer brief, qualification rubric, ownership matrix, meeting-outcome definitions, proof library, SLA, and fulfillment-capacity model.

**Do not proceed when:** the offer depends on a guarantee, owner conflicts are hidden, meeting outcomes are not recorded, or the delivery team cannot support the proposed volume.

### Cold-Calling Operations for SDRs

**Purpose:** turn verified prospects into qualified conversations and properly prepared meetings, not maximize dial count.

**Recommendations:**

- Call only `call_ready` records with verified company identity, relevant stakeholder or referral reason, normalized phone, suppression clearance, owner, source, and reason for contact.
- Use a short pre-call review: company, role, verified observation, prior activity, current opportunity context, and approved next step.
- Follow a consistent call structure: permission, relevance, discovery, reflection, qualification, next step, and immediate CRM closeout.
- Ask one question at a time and distinguish confirmed problems from assumptions.
- Book the sales assessment only when the prospect has a relevant problem, influence or referral authority, reasonable timing, and no disqualifier.
- Use approved responses for objections, voicemail, no-answer, wrong-person, opt-out, and nurture situations. Never invent a diagnosis or promise approval, ban prevention, performance, or revenue.
- Record the outcome, problem, interest, objection, referral, next action, and due date before moving to the next contact.
- Stop automated and manual outreach immediately when a prospect requests removal, and preserve the suppression state across all channels.
- Coach weekly using meaningful conversation rate, qualified-booking rate, show rate, SQL rate, wrong-person rate, invalid-contact rate, suppression errors, and note quality.

**Required outputs:** SDR call guide, qualification rubric, disposition dictionary, call-note template, follow-up SLA, coaching scorecard, and queue-quality report.

**Do not proceed when:** the contact is not verified, the phone is ambiguous, a suppression check is unavailable, or the SDR cannot explain the offer without unsupported claims.

### Before every call: 60-second preparation

Review only the information needed to sound informed:

- Contact name, role, company, website, segment, and location.
- Why the contact is in the queue and the source of that qualification.
- One verified business observation, if available.
- Current opportunity stage, owner, prior notes, and last touch.
- The approved booking calendar and the sales owner's Pacific Time availability.

Do not call when the record has a DNC, DND, unsubscribe, complaint, hard-bounce, opted-out, or equivalent suppression indicator. Do not invent a business problem from an incomplete record. If the observation is not verified, use the general problem statement.

### Recommended call structure

Keep the first call to roughly 3-8 minutes unless the prospect wants to continue.

1. **Permission:** identify yourself and ask whether 30 seconds is acceptable.
2. **Relevance:** explain the regulated-acquisition problem in one sentence.
3. **Discovery:** ask two or three questions, one at a time.
4. **Reflect:** summarize what you heard and check that it is accurate.
5. **Next step:** book the sales owner if qualified, send a checklist if not ready, or close respectfully.
6. **CRM closeout:** select the correct outcome, write the note, and set the next task before moving on.

### Core cold-call script

**Opening**

> Hi {{first_name}}, this is {{sdr_name}} from Transparent eCom. Did I catch you with 30 seconds?

If yes:

> We work with regulated businesses that are trying to improve paid acquisition but run into policy risk, inconsistent lead quality, or gaps between the ad and the sales follow-up. I was calling to see whether that is relevant for {{company}} right now.

If no:

> No problem. Is there a better time, or should I send a short checklist and close the loop?

**Optional verified observation**

> I noticed {{specific_verified_observation}}. I may be missing context, so I wanted to ask rather than assume: how are you currently handling {{related_business_problem}}?

**Discovery**

> What is the main acquisition issue you are trying to solve this quarter?

After the answer:

> How is that affecting qualified leads, booked calls, or sales opportunities today?

Then:

> What have you already tried, and what happened?

If there is a possible fit:

> Who else is involved in deciding whether to change this?

**Bridge to the sales owner**

> Based on what you shared, it sounds worth a closer look. Our sales owner leads the regulated acquisition work. They can review the current setup, explain the main constraint, and tell you whether there is a practical next step. Would you be open to a 20-minute Regulated Acquisition Assessment?

**Booking close**

> I can send you the sales owner's calendar now. It is in Pacific Time. Would you prefer an earlier or later time?

After booking:

> You are confirmed for {{date}} at {{time}} Pacific Time. You will receive the confirmation and reschedule information. I will also make sure the sales owner has the context we discussed.

### Generic script when no specific failure is known

> I do not want to pretend I have audited your account. The common issues we help regulated teams review are policy and claim risk, landing-page message match, lead routing, and source-to-revenue tracking. Are any of those currently a priority for {{company}}?

This is the default script. A specific diagnosis requires current evidence and approval.

### Qualification guide

Use the questions conversationally. Do not interrogate the prospect or force every question into the first call.

**Minimum fit signals**

- The company operates in a regulated segment Transparent eCom serves.
- The contact can influence acquisition, marketing, or the buying decision.
- There is a current business problem or active growth initiative.
- The company can provide the access, information, budget, and approvals required for the work.
- The prospect accepts that platform approval, performance, and revenue are not guaranteed.

**Disqualifiers or nurture signals**

- The business is outside the approved segment or market.
- The person wants non-compliant, deceptive, or ban-avoidance tactics.
- There is no meaningful acquisition need or timing.
- The contact is not involved and cannot introduce the decision-maker.
- The required budget, access, or operational readiness is clearly unavailable.

**Simple qualification questions**

- What do you sell, and which market are you serving?
- Which channel is most important to improve right now?
- Are you dealing with approval issues, weak lead quality, low conversion, or follow-up gaps?
- What would a useful improvement look like in the next 90 days?
- What has to be true internally before you can act?

Do not promise a result during qualification. The goal is to decide whether the sales owner's assessment is a sensible next step.

### Objection handling

Use the pattern `acknowledge -> clarify -> answer briefly -> offer a choice`.

**“We already have an agency.”**

> That makes sense. We are not trying to replace a good partner by default. Is your current agency also handling the regulated policy and lead-routing side, or is there one gap you still want solved?

**“Send me information.”**

> Absolutely. Which would be more useful: the five-point checklist or a short explanation of how the assessment works? If it looks relevant, you can decide later whether to speak with the sales owner.

**“We are not spending right now.”**

> Understood. Is the issue budget, timing, or uncertainty about what would work? I can note that correctly and avoid sending irrelevant follow-up.

**“How much does it cost?”**

> It depends on the scope and current setup. The sales owner can first determine whether there is a real problem to solve and what level of work is appropriate. I do not want to quote a package that does not fit.

**“Can you guarantee approval or prevent bans?”**

> No. Platform decisions depend on the platform, product, market, account history, and claims. We can review risk and build a more disciplined process, but we cannot guarantee approval or account survival.

**“We tried this before and it did not work.”**

> That is useful context. What failed: approval, lead quality, conversion, follow-up, or the commercial result? The sales owner can help determine whether the same constraint is still present before recommending anything.

**“I am not the right person.”**

> Thanks for telling me. Who owns regulated acquisition or paid growth there? If you prefer, I can send a short note you can forward rather than continuing to call you.

**“Not interested.”**

> Understood. I will not press. Should I close the loop entirely, or would it be reasonable to check back later in the year?

If the prospect asks to stop, mark the correct suppression state immediately and do not continue the conversation with another channel.

### Voicemail script

Keep it under 20 seconds and do not make a dramatic claim.

> Hi {{first_name}}, this is {{sdr_name}} from Transparent eCom. We help regulated businesses review paid acquisition, policy risk, and lead follow-up. I will send a short note with context. If this is relevant, you can reply there or choose a time with the sales owner. Again, this is {{sdr_name}} at {{callback_number}}.

Do not leave a second voicemail manually when the approved GHL voicemail action has already played. Record the outcome and let the configured follow-up process run.

### No-answer and follow-up guide

- `Voicemail`: use only when voicemail was reached; select the approved outcome and do not duplicate the drop.
- `Not Answered`: the phone rang without a live conversation or voicemail.
- `Busy`: use only for a carrier or phone-system busy signal.
- `Canceled`: use when the call was canceled before meaningful connection; document technical causes.
- `Completed`: use only after a meaningful live conversation, even if the prospect declines.

For an interested but unbooked prospect:

> I will send the short summary now. If the issue is still active, reply with a good time or use the sales owner's calendar. I will follow up on {{date}} and then leave the decision with you.

Do not chase indefinitely. Use a maximum of the approved attempt sequence, then move the record to the correct unresponsive or nurture state.

### SDR daily operating rhythm

**Before the calling block:** prepare the queue, remove suppressed records, review the day's priority accounts, and confirm the approved caller ID.

**During the block:** work one queue in order, keep notes open, use the script as a guide rather than reading mechanically, and disposition each call immediately.

**After the block:** complete all notes, create dated tasks, move qualified opportunities forward, flag ownership or routing errors, and report recurring objections.

### SDR scorecard

Review weekly by SDR and segment:

- Attempts completed.
- Meaningful conversations.
- Positive or qualified conversations.
- Meetings requested.
- Qualified meetings booked with the sales owner.
- Showed and no-showed meetings.
- SQLs created.
- MQL-to-SQL conversion.
- Qualified pipeline value.
- Average time from assignment to first attempt.
- Notes completed and next-task compliance.
- Wrong-person, correction, unsubscribe, DNC, and complaint rates.

Do not reward raw dials when they produce poor-fit meetings, complaints, inaccurate notes, or suppression failures. The manager should review a small sample of recorded notes and calls each week for accuracy, respectfulness, and correct disposition.

### SDR coaching loop

Every Friday, review:

1. The three most common objections.
2. The three calls that became qualified meetings.
3. One lost or poor-fit call.
4. One example of a clear note and one note needing correction.
5. Whether the problem is list quality, script relevance, booking friction, or qualification.

Change one part of the talk track at a time. Do not change scripts, audience, offer, and queue rules simultaneously because the team will not know what caused the result.

### SDR safety rules

- Follow applicable calling, recording, consent, and do-not-call requirements for the contact's location.
- Use the approved caller ID and approved voicemail recording.
- Never claim to have audited an account when no audit occurred.
- Never promise approval, account stability, revenue, or a launch date.
- Never call a suppressed contact or continue after a clear opt-out.
- Never use a different channel to bypass a reply, opt-out, or DNC request.
- Never change ownership, stages, tags, or workflow settings to hide an error.

## Emerald and Apollo Prospect Selection Plan

Emerald and Apollo should be treated as different sources with different jobs. Emerald is a campaign and audience source with useful segment/person-type buckets. Apollo is an enrichment and research source with person, company, title, employee, revenue, domain, and phone fields. Neither source alone proves that a business is a good client or that a person is the buyer.

Do not pull every available record into the SDR queue. Pull candidates into a review pool, filter them, verify them, and only then create a bounded `call_ready` queue.

### Emerald: what to pull

Use Emerald when the source record already represents a relevant regulated-business segment or person type. Prioritize these buckets:

1. `executives_mso`: owners, executives, or decision-makers at multi-location or multi-state operators.
2. `executives_sso`: owners or executives at single-state or single-operator businesses with a credible growth need.
3. `marketing_mso`: marketing leaders at multi-location or multi-state operators.
4. `marketing_sso`: marketing leaders at single-operator businesses.

For an SDR cold-call pilot, pull only records with:

- A current company name and a resolvable company domain.
- A first and last name, not only a generic inbox.
- A role matching executive, owner, marketing, growth, ecommerce, paid media, or a plausible referral path.
- A source bucket that matches the approved segment.
- A usable phone or a documented phone-repair task.
- No `Seq Enrolled - Emerald`, prior campaign completion, active campaign overlap, reply, DNC, DND, unsubscribe, complaint, hard-bounce, active customer, or active opportunity exclusion.

Do not use the Emerald release dispatcher as the SDR call-list generator. Its purpose is email enrollment and release logging. Create a separate reviewed SDR cohort so calling does not accidentally enroll or duplicate contacts in the Emerald email sequence.

### Emerald: what to exclude

Exclude or route to research when:

- The record has only a generic role such as info, support, admin, sales, careers, press, or wholesale.
- The company cannot be found, the domain is dead, or the domain does not match the business name.
- The person appears to be a vendor, consultant, recruiter, assistant, intern, or unrelated employee.
- The record is a duplicate of another person at the same company in the pilot.
- The business appears consumer-only, closed, inactive, outside the approved geography, or outside the selected segment.
- The source bucket is ambiguous or the person type cannot be confirmed.
- The company has no visible acquisition, ecommerce, retail, brand, or growth activity and no stated reason to discuss marketing.
- The contact has a missing or invalid phone and no approved alternate contact path.

An Emerald tag is an audience label, not a qualification result. Re-verify the company and person before calling.

### Apollo: what to pull

Use Apollo to find or enrich the best stakeholder at a company already passing the company screen. Start with a narrow search, not a database-wide export.

**Company filters:**

- Industry or keywords clearly connected to the approved regulated segment.
- Target geography and operating market.
- Active company domain and current website.
- Employee range and revenue range when available and credible.
- At least one signal of commercial activity: multiple locations, ecommerce, retail distribution, product catalog, current advertising, expansion, hiring, or an established sales/marketing function.

**Person filters:**

- Current title includes owner, founder, CEO, president, COO, chief marketing, marketing, growth, revenue, ecommerce, digital, acquisition, or paid media.
- Seniority is owner, partner, C-level, VP, director, head, or manager with a plausible path to the decision-maker.
- Work email uses the company domain where possible.
- Direct phone is preferred; mobile or direct business line is better than a switchboard.
- LinkedIn or company evidence supports the person's current role.

**Apollo fields to retain for review:**

- Apollo person and company IDs.
- Full name, title, seniority, department, and LinkedIn URL.
- Company name, domain, industry, geography, employee count, and annual revenue when available.
- Direct phone, phone type, email, email status, and enrichment date.
- Company LinkedIn URL, website, locations, and source/search date.

Apollo enrichment should improve identity and routing. It should not be used to claim that a prospect has a specific advertising failure or budget unless that fact is independently verified.

### Apollo filters that should not be used alone

Do not make a record call-ready merely because it has:

- A large employee count.
- A revenue estimate.
- An “interested” or engagement score.
- A job title containing marketing.
- A phone number returned by enrichment.
- An email that has opened a prior message.
- A scraped industry keyword.

These are prioritization clues. The company, person, role, phone, and business need still require review.

### Budget and client-value screening

Do not reject a business solely because it is small. Instead, estimate whether the potential client has a problem and enough commercial capacity for the proposed service to make sense.

Use these budget proxies in combination:

- Number of locations, markets, or brands served.
- Product breadth, ecommerce quality, retail distribution, or wholesale footprint.
- Current paid advertising or a clear intent to start or scale it.
- Dedicated marketing, growth, ecommerce, or paid-media staff.
- Recent expansion, hiring, funding, product launch, or market entry.
- Website quality, conversion path, analytics maturity, and CRM usage.
- Verified revenue or employee band, treated as directional rather than exact.
- Ability to name a business outcome and a realistic timeline.
- Ability to provide account access, creative approvals, product information, and a decision-maker.

Use a simple score, not a false precision model:

| Signal | 0 points | 1 point | 2 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial scale | No evidence | One location or small footprint | Multiple locations, brands, or markets |
| Marketing readiness | No owner or activity visible | Basic activity or one responsible person | Active team, agency, or paid acquisition program |
| Growth trigger | None found | General growth language | Expansion, launch, hiring, or active acquisition need |
| Ability to act | No access, budget, or timeline | Unclear | Decision-maker, timeline, and resources are plausible |
| Acquisition complexity | Little to diagnose | One channel or basic funnel | Regulated multi-channel, approval, lead-quality, or tracking complexity |

Starting interpretation:

- **8-10:** priority research and possible call-ready candidate.
- **5-7:** needs human review and a clear reason to call.
- **0-4:** do not place in the SDR calling queue; nurture, research, or disqualify.

These are internal prioritization thresholds, not claims about what a company can afford. The SDR must confirm need and timing conversationally.

### Fast low-spend and low-value exclusions

Move a record out of the immediate calling queue when most of the following are true:

- One small local operation with no visible expansion or paid acquisition activity.
- No marketing, ecommerce, growth, or owner contact can be identified.
- No credible website, product catalog, retail presence, or active business signal.
- No stated acquisition problem and no reason to act this quarter.
- No plausible ability to provide access, approvals, content, or implementation resources.
- The likely engagement would be too small for the approved delivery model or would require disproportionate support.

Do not make “low revenue” the only reason for exclusion. A smaller operator with a serious regulated acquisition problem, clear decision-maker, appropriate budget, and strong fit may be more valuable than a large company with no need or authority.

### Selection query logic in plain English

The first version of the source-selection rule should be:

```text
SELECT candidate
FROM one_approved_source
WHERE company_is_active
  AND company_domain_is_valid
  AND segment_is_approved
  AND stakeholder_role_is_relevant
  AND phone_or_approved_contact_path_is_present
  AND suppression_checks_pass
  AND no_active_campaign_overlap
  AND no_duplicate_company_in_current_cohort
  AND commercial_fit_score >= approved_threshold
ORDER BY intent_signal DESC, commercial_fit_score DESC, data_confidence DESC
LIMIT approved_cohort_size
```

The actual implementation must preserve the source, filter version, reviewer, and exclusion reason. Do not hide excluded records; report counts by reason so poor source quality can be corrected.

### Recommended source workflow

1. Select one Emerald bucket or one Apollo company search tied to one approved segment.
2. Export only the fields needed for screening and identity resolution.
3. Normalize email, phone, domain, company name, and person name.
4. Deduplicate by company domain first, then company name plus location, then person email/phone.
5. Apply suppression and active-campaign exclusions.
6. Verify the company, stakeholder, and commercial-fit signals.
7. Enrich missing phone or role data through Apollo only for promising records.
8. Send uncertain records to `needs_research`; never use enrichment as an automatic pass.
9. Create one primary contact per company and assign the correct SDR.
10. Manually review 25 records, then release only 10-15 `call_ready` contacts for the first pilot.
11. Compare wrong-person, invalid-contact, meaningful-conversation, qualified-booking, show, and SQL rates by source.
12. Keep, revise, or stop the source based on qualified outcomes, not contact volume.

### Source-quality decision rules

- High invalid-phone rate: stop calling that source and repair the data or change the phone filter.
- High invalid-email rate: stop email enrollment from that source and audit export freshness.
- High wrong-person rate: improve title/seniority/company-role filters before adding more records.
- High low-spend or no-need rate: narrow industry, scale, location, growth-trigger, or commercial-fit filters.
- High suppression or overlap rate: reconcile the source against the campaign controller before reuse.
- Good conversations but few bookings: review offer, script, and booking friction rather than pulling more contacts.
- Good bookings but poor shows or SQLs: improve qualification and handoff rather than increasing call volume.

The source is successful only when it produces verified stakeholders who become qualified, attended meetings with the sales owner at an acceptable delivery and compliance cost.

## SDR Business Knowledge Training

This is the minimum business knowledge an SDR needs before cold calling. The SDR does not need to be a media buyer or compliance lawyer. The SDR does need to explain Transparent eCom accurately, recognize a fit, and avoid promises the delivery team cannot control.

### What Transparent eCom does

Transparent eCom helps regulated businesses improve customer acquisition when ordinary advertising is restricted, inconsistent, or difficult to measure. The work can include:

- Regulated-ads and claim-risk review.
- Account, domain, tracking, and measurement planning.
- Compliant creative and landing-page strategy.
- Lead qualification, routing, CRM, and follow-up improvement.
- Reporting that connects marketing activity to qualified opportunities.

## Email Strategy Review

Email needs a strategy reset before more volume. The current system combines a high-volume newsletter, DAN and Emerald cold campaigns, Partnership outreach, event tracking, and GHL follow-up behavior. These should be treated as separate products with separate audiences, promises, cadences, suppression rules, and success metrics.

### Email diagnosis

The present risks are:

- Activity is being measured more confidently than qualified pipeline. Opens are weak evidence because mailbox privacy features inflate or distort them.
- The previous baseline of 43,181 emails in 30 days produced only 7 calendar bookings in the same reported window. This requires funnel and attribution review before sending more.
- The newsletter can send at a theoretical maximum of 6,999 messages per day across three senders, while the runbook notes that 2,333 per sender per day is approximately eight times the documented 300/day warm-up cap. This is a deliverability risk, not a growth strategy.
- Newsletter, DAN, Emerald, and Partnership emails use different sender/domain patterns. That can fragment reputation and confuse recipients if the identity and promise are not clear.
- The audit returned zero recent executions for several email workflows and the event-ingest workflow, which conflicts with historical send-log evidence. This discrepancy must be reconciled before making any volume decision.
- GHL DND suppression is causing a meaningful share of newsletter rejects. Those contacts should be permanently suppressed from marketing sends rather than retried or moved to another sender.
- Email Event Ingest and source attribution must be proven healthy. If the system cannot reliably capture delivery, replies, unsubscribes, complaints, bookings, and revenue, optimization is guesswork.

### Recommended email portfolio

Use four distinct email tracks:

1. **Newsletter:** one useful educational email per week to opted-in, eligible contacts. The goal is trust and qualified downstream action, not immediate sales from every issue.
2. **Segmented cold outreach:** one prospect, one problem, one relevant observation, and one low-friction reply CTA. Use a short sequence and stop when there is no signal.
3. **Behavioral follow-up:** triggered by booking, reply, meeting outcome, proposal, download, or high-intent page action. These emails should be operational and timely.
4. **Reactivation:** a low-frequency permission-based check-in for previously engaged but inactive contacts. Do not keep inactive contacts in every campaign indefinitely.

Do not enroll a contact in multiple marketing tracks at once. A campaign controller should assign one primary track, record the reason, and suppress competing campaigns until the track completes or the contact replies.

### What to send and how often

| Track | Audience | Recommended cadence | Primary success metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| Newsletter | Opted-in contacts with a valid business relationship or lawful outreach basis | 1/week, with a 2-week pause after repeated non-engagement | Qualified replies, booking rate, unsubscribes, complaints |
| Cold outbound | One verified ICP segment and role | 4 emails over 10 business days, then stop | Positive reply and qualified-booking rate |
| High-intent follow-up | Recent reply, booking, page action, or qualified handoff | Same day, then 1-2 reminders based on event | Show rate and next-step completion |
| No-show recovery | Contacts whose appointment outcome is no-show | Same day, next business day, and final check 3-5 business days later | Reschedule rate |
| Post-meeting | Showed meeting or proposal-stage opportunity | Same day recap, 2-day action reminder, 7-day decision check | SQL progression, proposal acceptance, close rate |
| Reactivation | Previously engaged contacts with no recent activity | 3 emails over 14 days, then suppress for 60-90 days | Positive reply, re-booking, unsubscribe rate |

### Platinum contact pilot

The Platinum-tagged GHL contacts are the best place to test this approach, but the Platinum tag must be treated as a **candidate cohort**, not as proof that every record is a verified business or an appropriate recipient.

#### Why Platinum should be different

Platinum contacts may have higher relationship value, but the list may contain:

- Real businesses that have not been reviewed recently.
- Duplicate contacts or multiple people at the same company.
- Consumers, vendors, agencies, or unrelated businesses.
- Contacts with incomplete company, role, domain, or consent information.
- Old contacts whose company, role, email, or business status has changed.
- Existing customers, active opportunities, replies, unsubscribes, DND records, or prior campaign history.

Sending the same automated cold sequence to this mixed list would damage the value of the cohort and make the test impossible to interpret.

#### Platinum eligibility review

Before any Platinum email is sent, create a read-only cohort export or research view using the exact GHL tag name. Confirm the tag name and count from live GHL rather than relying on a repository snapshot.

Classify each record into one of these buckets:

1. **Verified business:** company, domain, role, segment, and email identity are credible.
2. **Needs research:** likely business, but one or more key fields require confirmation.
3. **Non-business or out of scope:** consumer, vendor, unrelated industry, or disqualified segment.
4. **Duplicate:** another contact at the same company already represents the pilot.
5. **Suppressed or active:** DNC, DND, unsubscribe, complaint, hard bounce, active opportunity, active conversation, or recent outreach.
6. **Unusable:** invalid email, ambiguous identity, missing company context, or no lawful outreach basis.

Only the **Verified business** bucket should be eligible for the first pilot. The Needs research bucket should go through the observation workflow and human review before any enrollment decision.

#### Platinum pilot size and process

Use a staged pilot:

- Review 25 Platinum records manually.
- Send to no more than 10-15 verified contacts in the first cohort.
- Review every reply, correction, unsubscribe, and delivery result.
- Expand to 25-50 contacts only if identity accuracy and suppression checks are clean.
- Do not exceed 100 contacts until the message and cohort quality are proven.

The first cohort should be one segment and one role wherever possible. Do not mix dispensaries, MSOs, brands, agencies, and unknown businesses in the same test.

### How to produce `{{specific, verified observation}}`

Do not leave this as a merge field that is filled from a generic AI guess at send time. The observation should be prepared before a contact enters the sequence and should be traceable to public or first-party evidence.

#### Observation sources

Use this evidence hierarchy:

1. **First-party company website:** current services, markets served, locations, booking flow, regulated product category, hiring page, or an explicitly stated growth initiative.
2. **Official company announcement:** a dated expansion, new location, acquisition, product launch, partnership, or leadership announcement on the company's own site or verified social page.
3. **Public advertising evidence:** a current Meta Ad Library entry or other platform-visible ad activity, only when the observation is directly visible and dated.
4. **Public professional profile:** a current LinkedIn company or executive post that gives a business-relevant trigger.
5. **Verified enrichment:** Apollo or GHL fields for role, company, industry, geography, employee range, revenue band, or contact identity. These are useful for routing and qualification but are not, by themselves, a reason to claim that a company has a specific marketing problem.

Do not use scraped personal information, inferred budget, unverified ad disapproval, guessed performance problems, or a generic statement such as “I saw you are growing.”

#### Good observation examples

These are examples of the format, not claims about any specific company:

- `Your site lists three dispensary locations in California and routes visitors to a general contact form rather than a location-specific consultation path.`
- `Your company announcement says you opened the Phoenix location in May 2026; I was curious how you are measuring qualified demand for the new market.`
- `Your public Meta Ad Library listing shows active product or brand campaigns; I wanted to ask whether the landing-page and lead-routing side is being measured through to sales.`
- `Your careers page is hiring a paid media manager; I thought the compliance and attribution checklist might be useful while that function is being built.`
- `Your LinkedIn company page recently announced expansion into Nevada; are regulated acquisition and local lead quality priorities for that market?`

Each observation should connect to a business question without asserting that the prospect is failing.

#### Observation record

Before enrollment, create an internal record containing:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Contact and company ID | Canonical GHL contact ID and normalized company/domain. |
| Observation text | One sentence, specific, neutral, and suitable for the recipient. |
| Source URL | Direct public URL, not only a search result. |
| Source type | Website, announcement, ad library, LinkedIn, or enrichment. |
| Observed date | Date the source was checked in Pacific Time. |
| Confidence | High, medium, or rejected. |
| Expiry | Usually 30-60 days; shorter for ads, hiring, and time-sensitive events. |
| Reviewer | Person or process that approved the observation. |
| Campaign and use status | Where it was used, or why it was not used. |

Store this in a controlled Postgres research table or equivalent campaign record, not in an uncontrolled prompt or an opaque AI output. Do not add large research blobs to the GHL contact record unless there is a clear operator use case.

#### Research workflow

The recommended future workflow is:

1. Select an eligible contact after identity, role, company, segment, consent, and suppression checks.
2. Resolve the canonical company domain and remove duplicate company records.
3. Fetch a small, bounded set of approved public sources.
4. Extract candidate facts with URL and timestamp.
5. Reject candidates that are old, ambiguous, promotional, personal, or unsupported.
6. Generate one neutral sentence from the retained fact.
7. Require human approval for the first production cohort and for any high-risk regulated claim.
8. Pass only approved observations to the email sequence.
9. If no approved observation exists, use the generic segment-specific version or do not enroll the contact. Never invent personalization.
10. Expire and re-research observations rather than reusing stale triggers indefinitely.

#### Recommended implementation

The existing Apollo enrichment path should remain the identity and firmographic source, not become the entire research system. A future `Email Research and Personalization` workflow can use bounded HTTP requests and a review queue to create observation records. It should not send email itself.

Minimum safeguards:

- Maximum source count and request budget per contact.
- Explicit allowlist of source domains and URL types.
- No login-gated or private content.
- No send when identity or source confidence is ambiguous.
- Human review for the first 50 records per segment.
- Audit trail of source, wording, reviewer, and expiry.
- Suppression and reply checks immediately before enrollment and immediately before sending.

Personalization quality should be measured by positive reply rate and correction rate. If recipients frequently reply that the observation is wrong, pause the research workflow even if open rates look strong.

## Email Infrastructure Recommendation

### SendGrid decision

**Recommendation: do not add SendGrid yet.** First fix measurement, sender authentication, workflow execution visibility, suppression, and message-market fit within the current GHL architecture.

SendGrid or another dedicated ESP becomes justified if one or more of these are true:

- GHL cannot provide acceptable delivery, bounce, complaint, or suppression visibility after the current audit.
- Transactional email must be isolated from marketing reputation and managed independently.
- The business needs advanced template versioning, list-unsubscribe headers, domain-level analytics, inbox placement monitoring, or dedicated IP controls that GHL cannot supply.
- A verified volume and compliance program exists with a deliverability owner.
- The team is prepared to maintain webhook ingestion, consent state, suppression synchronization, bounce handling, and unsubscribe parity across both systems.

Do not adopt SendGrid merely to send more cold email or bypass a sender limit. A second ESP adds another source of truth and can make suppression failures more dangerous. If adopted later, use it first for a clearly separated transactional or permission-based stream, not as an ungoverned parallel cold-outreach path.

### Sender and domain decision

**Recommendation: do not add more senders now.** There are already multiple sender identities across `.com`, `.co`, `.agency`, and `.org`. More senders will not fix weak response, poor targeting, or an unauthenticated domain.

Preferred structure:

- One stable sender identity for the newsletter family, using the best-authenticated domain.
- One consistent human sender for cold outreach, with a clear role and reply ownership.
- One separate transactional identity if necessary for booking and operational messages.
- Do not rotate domains to evade complaints, bounces, DND suppression, or platform limits.
- Do not use a sales-owner sender address across unrelated campaigns unless the sales owner is the genuine reply owner and the recipient expectation is consistent.

Before changing sender count, verify for every sending domain:

- Exactly one valid SPF record.
- One DMARC record with aligned policy and reporting.
- DKIM signing and alignment.
- From-domain and return-path behavior.
- List-Unsubscribe and visible unsubscribe behavior where applicable.
- Google Postmaster or equivalent reputation signals.
- Bounce, complaint, DND, and suppression synchronization.
- Domain-level volume and complaint trends.

The current newsletter runbook explicitly identifies the 2,333-per-sender daily cap as materially above the documented 300/day warm-up guidance. Until placement and reputation data justify it, reduce volume to a controlled ramp rather than adding domains.

**Email Event Ingest**

- Verify GHL webhook delivery, endpoint authentication, event payload shape, and recent execution history.
- Add a heartbeat and alert when no event is received during an expected period or when failures exceed threshold.
- Store provider message ID, contact ID, campaign, sender, event type, event time, and raw-event hash.
- Deduplicate events and preserve provider timestamps.
- Treat complaints, unsubscribes, hard bounces, and DND updates as immediate suppression events.

### New workflows recommended

Build these only after approval and live-state inspection:

1. **Email Program Controller:** one contact-level eligibility and enrollment service that prevents simultaneous campaign enrollment and records the authoritative suppression reason.
2. **Email Preflight and Health Monitor:** checks scheduled workflow execution, queue growth, sender/domain caps, authentication status, event-ingest freshness, failed-send rate, and stale claims; reports actionable alerts without sending.
3. **Reply and Intent Router:** normalizes replies into positive, objection, unsubscribe, not-now, out-of-office, wrong-person, and ambiguous; assigns a human owner and suppresses automation immediately where required.
4. **Booking Nurture Workflow:** sends confirmation, reminders, reschedule, and no-show recovery messages from appointment events using Pacific Time.
5. **Post-Meeting Follow-up Workflow:** creates recap, next-step, proposal, and stalled-opportunity reminders from CRM stage and task events.
6. **Reactivation Workflow:** selects only eligible, previously engaged contacts after a defined cooling-off period and removes them after the final permission check.
7. **Deliverability Ledger and Dashboard:** reports delivery, hard bounce, soft bounce, complaint, unsubscribe, DND, sender, domain, campaign, and downstream booking rates by cohort.
8. **Email-to-Revenue Attribution Bridge:** links message event -> contact -> appointment -> owner -> opportunity -> revenue, while exposing unknown/unattributed records instead of guessing.

No new workflow should be created if the same behavior already exists but lacks monitoring. First consolidate duplicate logic and document the canonical path.

## Email Experiment Plan

### Experiment 1: message-market fit

- One segment, one role, one offer, one CTA.
- Two materially different message angles, not cosmetic subject-line tests.
- 250-500 verified recipients per variant, subject to deliverability and consent limits.
- Measure positive replies, qualified meetings, show rate, and SQLs.
- Stop if complaint, unsubscribe, hard-bounce, or DND rates breach the approved guardrail.

### Experiment 2: CTA type

Compare a reply CTA such as `reply checklist` with a direct booking CTA. Use the same audience and offer. The goal is to learn whether the market needs a lower-friction conversation before a calendar booking.

### Experiment 3: proof versus education

Compare a verified case study against a practical checklist. Do not invent or imply results. The winning variant is the one that produces qualified next steps, not the most opens.

### Experiment 4: newsletter downstream value

Compare weekly educational issues by qualified reply, site return, booking, SQL creation, unsubscribe, and complaint. Retire topics that produce attention but no useful action after a sufficient cohort.

## No-Traction Decision Tree

Use this sequence when email is still not producing qualified pipeline:

### Step 1: verify infrastructure before changing copy

Pause scale and inspect if any of these are unknown:

- Actual workflow execution and send-log reconciliation.
- SPF, DKIM, DMARC, alignment, and sender reputation.
- Hard-bounce and complaint rates.
- Suppression and unsubscribe correctness.
- Reply and booking event capture.
- Contact-to-source and source-to-booking attribution.

If infrastructure is not trustworthy, fix it before interpreting performance.

### Step 2: diagnose list quality

If delivery is poor, reduce volume and clean the list. Verify role, company, segment, geography, business status, email validity, and lawful outreach basis. Do not add senders to compensate for a bad list.

### Step 3: diagnose message and offer

If delivery is healthy but positive replies are below the agreed threshold after at least 500 verified deliveries in one segment, change the offer or message angle, not the sender count. Interview or manually review a sample of recipients and identify whether the problem is relevance, credibility, timing, or unclear next step.

### Step 4: diagnose booking friction

If positive replies exist but bookings are low, inspect response time, CTA friction, calendar availability, booking page load, timezone display, qualification form length, and follow-up. Use a reply CTA or assisted booking rather than sending more cold emails.

### Step 5: diagnose meeting quality

If bookings exist but shows or SQLs are low, fix qualification, confirmation, reminders, sales preparation, and offer fit. Do not increase top-of-funnel volume.

### Step 6: stop or pivot

After two materially different message/offer tests and at least 1,000 verified deliveries per segment without qualified traction, stop that segment sequence for 30-90 days. Reassess ICP, offer, proof, pricing, and channel rather than continuing automated follow-ups.

### Guardrail thresholds for approval

Use these as starting internal thresholds, to be finalized with deliverability advice and provider data:

- Hard bounce above 2%: pause the cohort and clean the list.
- Complaint rate at or above 0.1%: pause the campaign and investigate immediately.
- Unsubscribe or DND rate materially above the recent program baseline: stop the message variant and review relevance/consent.
- Delivery or event-ingest failure: treat performance as unknown, not zero.
- Positive reply rate below 0.5% after a verified 500-recipient segment test: review list, offer, and message before scaling.
- Qualified booking rate below 1 per 500 verified deliveries after two variants: stop scaling and change the funnel or segment.

These are decision triggers, not promises or industry benchmarks. Record the actual baseline and adjust only with evidence.

### Channel experiment rules

Every experiment must have:

- One hypothesis.
- One segment.
- One offer and CTA.
- A fixed start and stop date.
- A bounded audience or spend limit.
- A control or prior-period comparison where practical.
- Success and failure thresholds.
- Suppression, compliance, and rollback criteria.
- A decision owner.

No live outbound test should be run only to see whether a workflow works. Use pinned, dry-run, simulated, or explicitly approved low-volume tests according to the channel safety rules.

## Measurement System

### North-star metrics

The primary business scorecard should be:

1. Qualified meetings booked.
2. Showed meetings.
3. SQLs created.
4. Qualified pipeline value.
5. Closed-won revenue and gross profit.
6. Time from lead creation to first human response.
7. Time from meeting to next step.

Supporting metrics include channel cost, deliverability, reply rate, conversion rate, no-show rate, sales-cycle duration, churn, and client outcomes.

### Funnel definitions

Approve and document these definitions before comparing channels:

| Stage | Recommended definition |
|---|---|
| Contact | Deduplicated person or business record with a valid canonical ID. |
| MQL | The approved MQL stage or forward-looking `mql` tag ledger, reported separately until reconciled. |
| SQL | Sales Outreach opportunity created in the period, grouped by authoritative owner. |
| Booked meeting | Appointment on the canonical calendar, counted by local `start_at`. |
| Showed | Appointment outcome explicitly recorded as showed. |
| No-show | Appointment outcome explicitly recorded as no-show. |

| Won | Closed-won opportunity with recorded value and owner. |

### Required coverage indicators

The report must show, not hide:

- Owner coverage for opportunities and appointments.
- Lead-source attribution coverage for MQLs and SQLs.
- Booking-to-contact match rate.
- Appointment outcome completion rate.
- Duplicate and suppressed contact rate.
- Channel ledger coverage and freshness.
- Unknown or unattributed buckets.

An unattributed row is preferable to false precision.
